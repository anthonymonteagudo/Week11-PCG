Week 11: Proceduralism & Unreal PCG

Explore how reusable rules give artists control over generated content. Investigate Unreal Engine’s Procedural Content Generation (PCG) framework through point data, sampling, placement rules, and reusable graphs. Examine how these systems support environment creation, modular assets, and artist workflows.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how a PCG graph reads inputs, creates points, applies rules, and spawns meshes.
  • Describe how landscape, mesh, and spline sampling provide data for procedural placement.
  • Inspect point data and debug intermediate graph results to understand why placements survive or are removed.
  • Use parameters, seeds, graph instances, and asset presets to support artist control and reusable variations.
  • Evaluate generated results for visual quality, clearance, performance, and intended use.

Getting Started

Open Unreal/Week11PCGTrail/Week11PCGTrail.uproject in Unreal Engine 5.7 and let shaders finish compiling. The project opens PCG_Museum. Use Tools > Week 11 PCG Museum to navigate the prepared examples and open their graphs. Create a working copy of a map before experimenting.

While the project loads, consider: What repetitive task would you want an artist to control through a procedural tool?

In-Class Activities

Guest Lecture: Intro to PCG
Join Amritha Immaneni to explore procedural systems through data, rules, and constraints. Follow examples of grass placement on a landscape, foliage placement on a sampled mesh, and artist control through PCG Data Assets and Assemblies. Discuss memory, performance, and production examples.

Procedural Tools in Production
Review GOLF+ road and course generators. Discuss which decisions belong in reusable rules and which controls an artist needs to guide the result.

Points, Splines & Exclusions
Inspect points before spawning meshes. Compare placement along an open spline with sampling inside a closed region. Use Difference to remove unwanted placements, and account for asset size when checking clearance around paths and other objects.

Reusable Graphs & Modular Buildings
Explore how one placement graph supports different biome presets and asset profiles. Examine how a modular building’s walls, posts, and roof respond to changes in footprint and floor count.

Performance & Broader Applications
Distinguish editor generation time, runtime generation cost, and the rendering cost of generated assets. Discuss procedural level design, Houdini workflows, and possible applications to your final project.

Optional Guided Practice
Open Full trail exercise from the museum menu and select PCG_Scrub. Predict the effect of lowering KeepFraction from 0.78 to 0.50, regenerate, and compare from the same viewpoint. Restore the original value and explain why the tree and rock layers remain unchanged.

Predict → Change → Generate → Inspect

Change one input or control at a time and inspect both the points and the final meshes. Repeatable variation depends on keeping the other inputs, rules, assets, and settings consistent. Generated results still need artistic and technical review: a clear-looking path does not establish correct collision or navigation, and generating or baking assets does not eliminate their rendering cost.
